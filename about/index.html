How I Got Into Security01

This is a long story, so buckle up...

Long, long ago, when I was but a child, my mom got a new laptop, making her old one the new "family computer". My brothers and I would play around on it like any young kids would, although back in my day, us kids spent more time outside than we did inside. So when we DID get to play on the laptop, it was very exciting.

I eventually stumbled across PowerShell and Internet Explorer, and quickly learned that I could type 'tree' into powershell and look like a hacker from the movies. That was the pinnacle of my technical expertise... until the day when I learned I could remotely shut down other computers on the network, which I promptly got in trouble for doing.

Fast forward a bit to 10-year-old Branson. For Christmas, my parents bought me a Kano computer. The model I got was essentially a Raspberry Pi, a keyboard + trackpad combo, a case (with a speaker accessory), and an HDMI cable. The device was running a custom image based on Raspberry Pi OS meant to teach kids the basics of Linux, programming, and computers in general. I set it up on the TV at my grandparents house and fell in love.

It was on that device that I first learned the basics of Linux, Python, HTML, CSS, and more that I probably just can't remember. When I tell you that over the next 5 years I would spend HOURS upon HOURS messing around with the computer, it would be an understatement.

A couple years later I was playing a Galaga-type game on my Nintendo DS and found a bug where if I saved a game at the same time I ejected the cartridge, then quickly put it back in, a debug menu would open. From here I was able to figure out how to make it so that the enemies would automatically die at the start of each new round, making me continuously advance to the next stage... Hacker Branson was born in that moment.

Throughout the rest of my childhood into high school, I continued to learn more about computers. If you could go back in time and see me in this stage at home, the odds are that I would probably be disassembling something random. I would take apart toys, electronics, pick locks, etc. As an underclassman in high school, I took a web development class which I breezed through. It was at this point that I started reading more about browser exploits and famous hackers... My interest was once again piqued.

Going into college, I was convinced I wanted to be a Penetration Tester, however the more classes I took at AU (such as Programming for Security, Network Security, Cyber Threat Intelligence, etc.), the more and more I fell in love with the defensive side of things. I got involved in the University's CTF program, which the puzzle-solver in me quickly fell in love with. Over the course of my college career I have both led and been a part of many top-ranking teams. I got involved with the Cybersecurity club originally as the Events Manager, which was the most incredible opportunity. Through that, I was pushed outside of my comfort zone and encouraged to talk to many professionals. It is in this time that I met the most influential people in my professional career thus far. From there, I was given the opportunity to run in the election for President of the club which I ended up winning. It has been a joy and a pleasure getting to pour back into the younger Cybersecurity majors, and I like to think of it as my little way to give back to the wonderful community that pushed me so hard to become who I am today.
